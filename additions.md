### Long & Thin branch

**Spaghetti alla chitarra**
- Parent: Long & thin → solid → thick strands
- Pronunciation: spah-GET-tee ah-la kee-TAR-rah
- Icon: 🎸
- Fact: Made by pressing dough through a wooden frame strung with guitar wires — each strand has a square cross-section, not round, which holds sauce differently than regular spaghetti.

**Pici**
- Parent: Long & thin → solid → thick strands
- Pronunciation: PEE-chee
- Icon: 🐛
- Fact: A hand-rolled pasta from Tuscany, each strand rolled by hand into an uneven thick rope. No two pieces are identical, and that irregularity is considered the point.

**Bigoli**
- Parent: Long & thin → hollow
- Pronunciation: BEE-goh-lee
- Icon: 🍝
- Fact: A thick hollow pasta from Venice, traditionally made with whole wheat or duck eggs, and historically the pasta of the poor — served on days when meat was forbidden.

### Long & Flat branch

**Mafaldine**
- Parent: Long & flat → wide, wavy edge
- Pronunciation: mah-fal-DEE-neh
- Icon: 👑
- Fact: Named after Princess Mafalda of Savoy — the ruffled edges were meant to resemble the lace trim of royal dresses.

**Scialatielli**
- Parent: Long & flat → medium width
- Pronunciation: shah-lah-tee-EL-lee
- Icon: 🌊
- Fact: Invented in 1978 by a single chef in Amalfi — one of the few modern pastas with a known inventor and a documented birth date.

**Pizzoccheri**
- Parent: Long & flat (add new sub-branch: buckwheat / dark)
- Pronunciation: peet-SOK-keh-ree
- Icon: 🏔️
- Fact: Made with buckwheat flour from the Valtellina valley in Lombardy — one of the very few Italian pastas not made from wheat. Traditionally baked with cabbage, potato, and melted cheese.

### Short & Tubular branch

**Paccheri**
- Parent: Short & tubular → smooth → large
- Pronunciation: PAK-keh-ree
- Icon: 🫁
- Fact: So large you can stuff individual tubes like small cannelloni. The name likely comes from a Neapolitan word for a slap — because of the slapping sound they make against the plate.

**Mezze maniche**
- Parent: Short & tubular → smooth → medium
- Pronunciation: MED-zeh MAH-nee-keh
- Icon: 👕
- Fact: Means "half sleeves" — a shorter, wider version of rigatoni. A Roman staple, almost always the pasta used in an authentic amatriciana.

**Calamarata**
- Parent: Short & tubular → smooth → large
- Pronunciation: kah-lah-mah-RAH-tah
- Icon: 🦑
- Fact: Shaped to mimic rings of calamari — traditionally served with actual squid so the pasta and the seafood are visually indistinguishable on the plate.

**Ditaloni**
- Parent: Short & tubular → small
- Pronunciation: dee-tah-LOH-nee
- Icon: 🔩
- Fact: A larger version of ditalini, the name means "big thimbles." The preferred pasta for pasta e fagioli in Naples — the thick tube traps beans inside it.

### Short & Twisted / Shaped branch

**Strozzapreti**
- Parent: Short & twisted → hand-rolled
- Pronunciation: stroht-tsah-PREH-tee
- Icon: 🙏
- Fact: Means "priest stranglers" — legend says local women made this pasta for the clergy as payment, and the priests ate so greedily they choked on it.

**Trofie**
- Parent: Short & twisted → tight spiral
- Pronunciation: TROH-fyeh
- Icon: 🌀
- Fact: The only pasta traditionally designed specifically for pesto — its tight twist and rough texture grip the sauce so well that Ligurians consider it almost a rule that trofie must be served with pesto genovese.

**Casarecce**
- Parent: Short & twisted → rolled/curled
- Pronunciation: kah-sah-REH-cheh
- Icon: 📜
- Fact: Each piece is a small sheet rolled into a loose S-scroll. The name means "homemade" — it was originally a pasta made only at home, never commercially produced.

**Cavatappi**
- Parent: Short & twisted → spiral tube
- Pronunciation: kah-vah-TAP-pee
- Icon: 🍾
- Fact: Means "corkscrews" — a hollow spiral that combines the sauce-trapping of a tube with the grip of a twist. Invented in the 1970s and now one of the most copied shapes globally.

**Mandala / Rotelle**
- Parent: Short & twisted → wheel-shaped
- Pronunciation: roh-TEL-leh
- Icon: ☸️
- Fact: Designed in the 1960s to mimic wagon wheels. Food engineers spent months perfecting the spoke-to-rim ratio so the whole piece cooks evenly — the spokes and rim are different thicknesses.

### Hand-shaped / Regional branch (NEW branch at top level)

Add a new top-level branch from the root node: **"Is it hand-shaped?"**

This branch covers pasta that is traditionally formed by hand without extrusion or a mold.

**Orecchiette**
- Pronunciation: oh-reh-kyeh-TEH
- Icon: 👂
- Fact: Means "little ears." In Bari, it is still made by street vendors who sit outside their homes dragging small rounds of dough across wooden boards with a butter knife — a technique passed from mother to daughter for generations.

**Cavatelli**
- Pronunciation: kah-vah-TEL-lee
- Icon: 🐚
- Fact: Made by pressing a finger along a small piece of dough and dragging it to create a curl — one of the oldest pasta-making gestures, essentially unchanged since the Middle Ages.

**Maltagliati**
- Pronunciation: mahl-tah-LYAH-tee
- Icon: 🗺️
- Fact: The name means "badly cut" — these are intentionally irregular triangles torn or cut from pasta sheets without any uniform shape. Imperfection is the entire aesthetic.

**Sagne 'mpastrate**
- Pronunciation: SAH-nyeh mpah-STRAH-teh
- Icon: 🌿
- Fact: A Calabrian pasta of hand-torn ribbons deliberately made uneven. The rough edges catch thick vegetable sauces in ways a clean-cut pasta cannot — the irregularity is functional, not accidental.

**Lorighittas**
- Pronunciation: loh-ree-GHEE-tahs
- Icon: 💍
- Fact: A Sardinian pasta made by twisting two strands together into a ring, then braiding the join. Traditionally made only on All Saints' Day — each ring takes about a minute to form by hand.

**Cencioni**
- Pronunciation: chen-CHOH-nee
- Icon: 🍃
- Fact: Means "little rags" — small irregular petal shapes from Tuscany, pressed with a thumb to create a curved, rough surface. Each piece looks slightly different from the last.

### Stuffed branch

**Agnolotti del plin**
- Parent: Stuffed → small, pinched
- Pronunciation: ah-nyoh-LOT-tee del PLIN
- Icon: 🤌
- Fact: "Plin" means "pinch" in Piedmontese dialect — each tiny parcel is sealed with a single pinch of the fingers. Traditionally filled with leftover roast meat, making it a pasta born from frugality.

**Cappellacci**
- Parent: Stuffed → larger, hat-shaped
- Pronunciation: kap-pel-LAH-chee
- Icon: 🎩
- Fact: Means "big ugly hats" — a larger, more rustic version of cappelletti from Ferrara. Traditionally filled with pumpkin and amaretti biscuits, a combination that sounds wrong and tastes extraordinary.

**Culurgiones**
- Parent: Stuffed → pinched/braided seal
- Pronunciation: koo-lur-JOH-nehs
- Icon: 🌾
- Fact: A Sardinian stuffed pasta sealed with an elaborate braided edge that looks like a wheat stalk. The folding technique is so specific that each village in Ogliastra has its own variation, and locals can identify which village made it by the braid pattern.

**Schlutzkrapfen**
- Parent: Stuffed → half-moon
- Pronunciation: SHLOOTS-krahp-fen
- Icon: 🏔️
- Fact: A half-moon pasta from South Tyrol, where Italian and Austrian traditions meet — the dough uses rye flour and the filling is spinach and ricotta. It sits on the border between pasta and dumpling, and locals debate which it is.
